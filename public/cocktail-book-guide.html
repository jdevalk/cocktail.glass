Collector acquisition plan

Recommended purchase strategy

Buy the great copies first

  1. Acquire the live 1862 Jerry Thomas. It is a first-edition later state in original cloth at $2,395. Ask for a complete collation, restoration statement and insured Netherlands shipping quote.
  2. Commission wants lists. Give two respected cocktail/culinary specialists the exact issue points for Johnson 1882, Thomas 1887, Savoy 1930 and Café Royal 1937.
  3. Buy jacketed interwar originals opportunistically. Baker, So Red the Nose, Waldorf, Embury and Trader Vic are condition-sensitive; the jacket can be most of the value.
  4. Buy modern first printings signed. Seek copies signed directly through author events, reputable dealers or documented publisher stock.

Rule: a blank space is preferable to a facsimile. Consolidate only when one good dealer happens to hold multiple correct copies.

Pre-purchase checklist

  1. Exact title-page and copyright-page photographs.
  2. Edition, printing and issue points stated in writing.
  3. Complete pagination and plate/illustration collation.
  4. Original binding and jacket status; all restoration disclosed.
  5. Ownership marks, inscriptions and provenance photographed.
  6. Dealer association and return terms verified.
  7. Insured, tracked shipping with rigid moisture-safe packing.

For expensive non-EU purchases, obtain a written landed-cost estimate. Taxes are a transaction detail, never a reason to choose an inferior copy.

First edition is not enough. For titles with multiple states or impressions—especially Jerry Thomas and The Savoy Cocktail Book—the page distinguishes the state actually offered. “First edition” without issue evidence is marked unverified.
